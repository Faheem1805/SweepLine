Problem statement
How might we advance the sustainable management of seasonal jellyfish blooms in coastal environments?

Early warning already exists. The challenge starts after it: manage large, continuous swarms with less manual effort, less specialised equipment and less field support, with no harm to marine life and no redesign of plant systems.

Proposed solution

Two full-depth nets stand across the intake channel at 20°, ahead of every intake. Water passes through. Jellyfish are held back, so the plant’s own flow sweeps them along the net to a collector. A slow screw lift sends them over the breakwater and 2 km down the coast. No boats chasing the bloom: crews only swap panels. Plant systems unchanged.

Technical explanation

2 million m³/h through a 140 × 12 m channel runs at 0.33 m/s. A 20° net splits that: 0.11 m/s through the mesh, inside the 0.12 m/s limit used for fish screens, and 0.31 m/s along it, so animals should slide, not pin. Any channel needs at least flow ÷ (depth × 0.12 m/s) of net. At 0.12 m of head the float line dips: cooling comes first.
